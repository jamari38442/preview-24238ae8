How to Stop Leash Pulling: A Step-by-Step Guide

First Option K95 min read

Dogs pull because pulling works. It gets them where they want to go faster. The fix is to make a loose leash the thing that pays off. Here is the approach we use, step by step.

1. Start with the right gear

Use a 6ft leash, not a retractable one, and a well-fitted harness or flat collar. A retractable leash teaches constant tension, which is the exact opposite of what you want.

2. Reward the position you want

Decide which side your dog walks on. Whenever they are near your leg with a loose leash, mark it with a word like "yes" and reward at your side. You are paying for the position, so pay it often at first.

3. Stop moving when they pull

The moment the leash goes tight, stop. No drama, no yanking. Wait for slack, then move again. Your dog quickly learns that pulling stops the walk and a loose leash starts it.

4. Change direction

If your dog locks onto something, calmly turn and walk the other way. This keeps you unpredictable and interesting, and it teaches your dog to check in with you instead of towing you around.

5. Practice short and often

Five focused minutes in a low-distraction spot beats a chaotic 45-minute walk. Build the skill where it is easy, then add distractions slowly.

In the South Florida heat, train early or late and keep sessions short. A hot, panting dog cannot focus, and hot pavement can burn paws.

When to get help

If your dog lunges or reacts to other dogs and people, that is more than pulling and it needs a behavior plan, not just leash mechanics. That is a great time to book an evaluation.
